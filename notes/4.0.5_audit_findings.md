## 🔴 New finding 1: Capacity-payment-keeps-plants-online (entirely missed)

**What I missed.** 4.0.5 introduces a real, behavior-altering mechanism that I did not document at all. The model now computes retirements *both* with and without predicted capacity-market revenue, and tracks the difference as `Generation Capacity Kept Online due to Predicted Capacity Payment`:

```
Generation Capacity Kept Online due to Predicted Capacity Payment
  = Cumulative Retired Generation Capacity
  - Cumulative Retired Generation Excluding Capacity Payment
```

In other words, plants that *would* have retired economically can be kept online if expected capacity-market revenue tips them past the profitability threshold. This is a real grid-economics phenomenon (e.g., a coal plant nearing retirement that gets a capacity-market contract for another 3 years) and is now reflected in the model.

**4.0.4** had no such variable; in 4.0.4 capacity-market revenue contributed to overall profit but the model didn't expose the "kept-online" delta as a tracked output.

**Where it lives.** New variables in `Electricity Supply - Main` and Cash Flow:
- `Retiring Generation Capacity Excluding Capacity Payment[Electricity Source]`
- `Retiring Generation Capacity including Economic and Policy Mandated Retirements Excluding Capacity Payment[...]`
- `Cumulative Retired Generation Capacity` and `Cumulative Retired Generation Excluding Capacity Payment`
- `Generation Capacity Kept Online due to Predicted Capacity Payment`

**Recommended doc impact.** Add a paragraph to `electricity-sector-main.md` "Economic, Policy-Driven, and Planned Retirements" subsection (where I already added the BUCLfCR economic-lifetime path) describing how predicted capacity-market revenue can keep a plant online beyond the point at which it would otherwise have retired economically. Worth at least one screenshot TODO.

## 🟠 Audit 2 — Capacity construction "split" framing wrong

**What my prose says** (in `electricity-sector-main.md` "Policy Mandated Capacity Additions and Retrofits"):
> "Generation capacity (per electricity source type) and grid battery storage are handled as separate paths, with their own BAU input schedules and their own non-BAU policy levers; this lets a user override mandated grid-battery additions without disturbing mandated generation construction, and vice versa."

**What's actually true:** The 4.0.4 `Boolean Use Non BAU Mandated Capacity Construction Schedule[Electricity Source]` lever was indexed by `Electricity Source`, and `Electricity Source` does NOT include grid battery storage as a value (it's a 24-element subscript covering generation technologies only). 4.0.4 had **no grid-battery mandate path at all**. 4.0.5 *adds* a parallel grid-battery-mandate as a new capability — it's not a "split" of an existing unified lever.

**Recommended fix:** Reframe as "4.0.5 *adds* a grid-battery mandate path alongside the existing generation mandate" instead of "splits". Phase 2 notes also need this correction (Theme 4 in `notes/4.0.4_to_4.0.5_changes.md`).

## 🟠 Audit 10 — Reliability "dispatchable-only" overstated structurally

**What my prose says** (in the rewritten "Reliability Additions" section):
> "Reliability additions in the EPS are restricted to **dispatchable resources** ... Variable renewable resources like solar PV and onshore wind are not added through this mechanism because their output during a given peak hour is determined by weather rather than by the operator's decision to dispatch them."

**What's actually true:** The 4.0.5 `Profitable Capacity for Seasonal Dispatchable Reliability Additions` is defined for the full `Electricity Source` subscript — there is no structural subscript restriction excluding variable renewables. Instead, the mechanism applies a `Last Year Hourly Bid Electricity Capacity Factors for Reliability in Binding Hour by Plant Type` weighting to the capacity-market revenue. For variable renewables, that capacity factor in the binding peak hour (typically a winter evening) is very low or zero, so they fail the profitability threshold and don't get added.

The 4.0.4 mechanism was structurally tighter — `Profitable Capacity for Seasonal Dispatchable Reliability Additions[Reliability Electricity Source within Electricity Source]` was scoped to a defined `Reliability Electricity Source` subset. 4.0.5 relaxed the structural subscript but the **behavior is the same** because variable renewables fail on availability rather than being ruled out by subscript.

**Severity:** small. The end-result framing ("variable renewables don't enter via reliability") is correct in practice. The issue is just that the prose claims a structural restriction that's actually behavioral.

**Recommended fix:** Change "are restricted to dispatchable resources" to something like "in practice add only dispatchable resources, because variable renewables receive minimal capacity-market revenue when their availability in the binding peak hour is low (e.g., solar in a winter evening peak)." Or leave as-is for readability — the everyday-reader-friendly framing matches the practical outcome even if technically loose.

## 🟡 Audit 4 — AVCC EV battery: new MW computation grafted onto unchanged MW·hr prose

**What my prose says** (in `electricity-sector-main.md` "EV Batteries"):
> "The model aggregates the total amount of battery capacity from the existing stock of vehicles -- specifically by multiplying the number of battery electric vehicles in each vehicle and cargo type by the average vehicle charger capacity (in kW per vehicle) for that combination -- and then applies a BAU share..."

**What's actually true:** The 4.0.4 `Maximum Daily Quantity of Electricity Charged by EVs for Diurnal Balancing` (in MW·hr units) is **byte-identical** in 4.0.5 — that pre-existing energy-budget calculation is unchanged. 4.0.5 *separately* computes a new `Total EV Battery Capacity Available in MW` (power-units) using the AVCC × Vehicles × Fraction formula. This new MW figure feeds a new `Total Demand Shifting Capacity in MW` aggregator (also new in 4.0.5) alongside demand response, pumped hydro, and grid+hybrid storage.

So the doc subsection is now describing two distinct things in the same paragraph: the existing daily energy budget AND the new instantaneous power capacity. My edit grafted the AVCC description onto the existing energy-budget paragraph without distinguishing them, which is slightly misleading — the AVCC computation is for a different quantity (MW vs. MW·hr).

**Severity:** small. The doc is still factually accurate, just confusing.

**Recommended fix:** Split the paragraph into two sentences making the two outputs distinct: (1) the daily MW·hr energy budget for diurnal balancing (unchanged from 4.0.4); (2) the new MW power-capacity aggregation that feeds `Total Demand Shifting Capacity in MW` for grid-balancing capability tracking.

## 🟡 Audit 3 — Data center load wording slightly imprecise

**What my prose says** (in `electricity-sector-main.md` "Calculating Hourly Electricity Demand from Annual…"):
> "Data-center load is added to the rest of the hourly final demand at this stage."

**What's actually true:** The data-center load is added at the **annual** demand level (alongside transportation, buildings, industry, district heat, hydrogen) inside `BAU Total Electricity Demand`, then converted to hourly via the `Adjusted Load Factors for Data Centers`. The conversion happens in the same section but at a slightly different point in the chain. Saying "added to the hourly final demand" is loose.

**Severity:** trivial. Most readers won't notice. The functional description is correct.

**Recommended fix:** "Data-center load is included in the annual electricity demand aggregation alongside the other sectors, and is converted to hourly using the data-center-specific load factors described above" — or leave as-is.

## Other broader 4.0.5 → 4.0.4 changes I want to flag for awareness

These are areas where I trust my prose to be accurate, but they involve broader cross-sector implications worth keeping in mind during your review.

### Broader change A — Vintage tracking is structurally pervasive

I documented this as Theme 7 (cash flow), but it actually has wider implications I didn't fully tease out:

- The new `Vintage:` subscript covers `(Vintage1891-Vintage2100)` — 210 vintage years.
- `Electricity Generation Capacity by Vintage[Electricity Source, Vintage]` is a level variable now tracked in the Main view, not just cash flow.
- The `Capacity Retirements Based on Economic Lifetime` mechanism uses vintage-age comparison; without vintage tracking, the BUCLfCR setting wouldn't have a way to know which plants reached their economic lifetime.
- The `Annual Cost Recovery for Historical Power Plant Construction Costs by Vintage` mechanism amortizes financing over the historical-vintage timeline.

**Implication:** Vintage tracking is not just a cash-flow accounting refinement; it's a foundational structural addition that enables the economic-lifetime retirement mechanism (Theme 6) and the cost-of-service rate computation (Theme 8). The doc currently mentions vintage in the cash flow context only; the main doc could reference it as the structural underpinning of those new mechanisms. Worth a brief callout in `electricity-sector-main.md` "Tracking the Electricity Fleet" subsection.

### Broader change B — Three new control-settings levers (not policy levers)

I noted these individually but didn't surface them as a pattern: 4.0.5 adds three new settings under `InputData/ctrl-settings/`:
- `BUCLfCR` — turn capacity-lifetime retirement on/off (default 0)
- `BEEEfEPS Boolean Exclude Elec Exports from Elec Portfolio Stds[Clean Elec Requirement]` — exclude exports from RPS/CES (per-CER)
- `DCLGA Data Center Load Growth Assumption` — select low/mid/high data-center trajectory

These are **model-control settings** (typically set once for a run) rather than user-facing policy levers (with implementation schedules). The distinction matters for documentation: control settings are closer to "configuration" than "policy choice." The docs don't currently make this distinction explicit anywhere. Could be worth a brief paragraph in `assumptions.md` or `creating-and-managing-scenarios.md`.

### Broader change C — Hybrid storage as first-class citizen

Multiple themes (battery dispatch refactor, capacity-construction split, grid battery storage refinements, endogenous learning naming cleanup) all touch on the standalone-vs-hybrid-battery distinction. 4.0.5 has elevated hybrid storage to a first-class concept with its own subscript (`Hybrid Electricity Source: {onshore wind es, solar PV es, offshore wind es}`) and dedicated dispatch machinery.

The docs now mention hybrid in several places but never give it a unified treatment. A reader who wants to understand "what's hybrid storage in the EPS" needs to assemble it from fragments across the main, cash flow, and endogenous-learning docs. Not necessarily a 4.0.5 fix — but worth flagging as a documentation-organization issue.

### Broader change D — Two missed framings I should not over-correct

For completeness:

- **The 4.0.5 RPS optimization is actually new.** 4.0.4 only had a CES optimization (`Optimized CES Credit Price`); RPS was a percentage-target without a separate optimization pass. 4.0.5 added the RPS optimization machinery as a parallel of the existing CES one. My Phase 3 prose says "the RPS and CES optimizations run in separate passes" which is correct for 4.0.5; the additional fact (this is a structural addition to the RPS-only pre-4.0.5 behavior) is not currently in the docs. Probably fine to leave since the unified-framework framing covers it, but flagging for completeness.

- **Buildings fuel-cost component subscript drop is a real subscript-range simplification** that I correctly flagged in Phase 2 notes but didn't tag prominently as "subscript change" per CLAUDE.md's structural-change scrutiny rule. The `Building Component` subscript was dropped from the cash-flow `Amount Spent on Buildings Fuels` chain. Since the upstream `Building Components Energy Use` retains the component dimension, this is a *projection* rather than a structural removal — the component-level data still exists, just isn't carried into cash flow. Treating this as a subscript-projection-not-removal is the right framing; my notes implicitly do this, but the SKILL.md "subscript changes deserve scrutiny" rule could have made me flag it more prominently.

## Audit lessons for the SKILL.md

The pattern I missed in the reliability case (Audit issue) and again in the capacity-construction-split case (#2 above) is the same: I read the variable-name diff, formed a hypothesis about what the change means, and described the change without tracing the actual *equation flow* end-to-end to confirm the hypothesis. The fix is to extend SKILL.md Step 7's holistic re-read to explicitly include "for every documented theme, run the equation diff on the variables that consume the changed variables, and verify the framing matches the actual structural flow."
